Integration of Telesurgery into society: does it bridge the healthcare gap?

Abstract

Our team investigates telesurgery where a surgeon operates on a patient from a different location using robotics and network technology, focusing on its potential to expand surgical access in low-income, conflict, and rural regions. Proven feasible by the 2001 Lindbergh Operation (Marescaux et al., 2002), telesurgery also presents critical socio-ethical challenges across levels of social analysis. Institutional equity is constrained by costly robotics and high-speed network dependencies (Dohler et al., 2024), while professional liability complicates doctor-patient relationships (Elendu et al., 2025). Ultimately, we examine whether telesurgery bridges healthcare gaps or deepens infrastructural inequality within a global and NZ context.
